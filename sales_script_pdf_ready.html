Aegis Protocol: Two-Call Sales Script & Straight Line Close

This document outlines the strict Two-Call Sales Process for selling Aegis Protocol to businesses.

PHASE 1: THE COLD CALL (Qualify & Book)

The Goal: Hit their pain point immediately, qualify them as a buyer, and book a follow-up meeting. Do not pitch the full product on this call. Keep it under 2 minutes.

1. The Introduction & Hook

"Hi, my name is [Your Name]. I am a Business Coach at Metrixmedia, a full-service digital marketing agency.

Reason for the call today, [Name]—we help local businesses stand out by building a 5-star online rating so they can attract more customers and grow their revenue.

I was checking your [Google Map / Zomato / Swiggy / Amazon / Flipkart] profile this morning, and if you have just 60 seconds, I'd like to show you how we can help you boost your online presence.

Got a minute?"

2. The Value Proposition

(Wait for their response. If they say "yes" or show interest, proceed:)

"Okay great. See, what we did is we built an in-house, cutting-edge rating software that can detect 1-star and 2-star reviews before a user even posts them. This can help your online reputation grow from your current rating up to 4.9 stars.

The best parts are:
* It is completely automatic, meaning you don't have to manually monitor it.
* It is easy to setup (takes roughly 5 minutes).
* And it is extremely affordable—costing less than a Starbucks coffee.

Before we set up a quick time to look at it, I just have a few quick questions to make sure this is actually a good fit for your business."

3. The Qualification

Question 1: Importance of Online Rating

"How important is your online rating for your business?"
(If they say it's not important, they might need educating. If important, proceed.)

Question 2: Rating Management History

"Are you currently, or have you in the past, used any service to manage your rating?"

Question 3: Agency Partnership

"Are you currently working with any digital marketing agency?"

(If yes, follow up with:)
  • "Oh, really? For what specific service are they helping you with?"
  • "And how has your experience been with them so far—are you actually seeing the results you paid for?"

Question 4: The Emotional Pain Point

"I noticed you have a few lower reviews online. Let me ask you—how does it feel when you put in so much hard work, and then someone leaves a 1 or 2-star review that drags your rating down?"
(Listen carefully. This sets up the pain.)

Question 5: Decision Maker

"By the way, am I speaking with the owner or the main decision maker in your business, or is there someone else you partner with on these decisions?"
(If no: "Got it. Who would be the best person to speak with, and when is a good time to reach them?")

4. Booking the Meeting (The Cliffhanger)

"Based on everything you just told me, you are a perfect fit for a system we built called the Aegis Protocol. It literally intercepts bad reviews before they hit the internet, and automatically funnels your happy customers to give you 5 stars.

I know you're busy running the business right now. What would be the best time tomorrow for a quick, 3-minute screen-share so I can show you exactly how it works?"

(Lock in the time and end the call).

PHASE 2: THE MEETING (Pitch & Close)

The Goal: Explain the Aegis mechanism clearly, ask for the order, and use the Straight Line Loops to handle objections.

1. The Pitch (The Aegis Protocol)

"Hey [Name], thanks for taking the time. As promised, I'll keep this to 3 minutes.

"Here is exactly how the Aegis Protocol works: We register your business on our high-tech protocol and generate a dynamic, active feedback link or QR code. When your customers click the link or scan the code, instead of going directly to Google Maps, they are redirected to our private portal where the protocol lives.

"It actively checks every single review. If someone tries to leave a 1 to 3-star review, the system intercepts it and converts it into private feedback. Every day, we email you a list of those bad reviews and the reasons behind them, so you can work to improve things internally.

"But those who want to give you 4 or 5 stars are instantly redirected to Google Maps, where they can publicly give you that 5-star rating for the whole world to see.

"It acts as a perfect middleman. If a customer is frustrated, our system catches them before they reach Google Maps. Nobody in the world will ever know that problem existed. But if they are a huge fan of your business, it funnels them straight to the top.

"So literally overnight, you stop bad reviews from going public, and you funnel all the good ones to your profile. This immediately jumps your rating to 5 stars and starts stealing customers from your competitors."

2. The Initial Close (Ask for the Order)

"The system pays for itself the second it intercepts one bad review or brings in one new customer. It's very affordable, and setup takes 5 minutes. Sound fair enough to get you set up today?"

(Wait for their response. If they say "yes", process the payment. If they give an objection like "I need to think about it" or "Send me an email", enter Loop 1 below).

THE CLOSING SEQUENCE (The 3 Straight Line Loops)

When you get an objection, it's almost always a smokescreen. Follow this exact sequence to knock out their uncertainties, lower their risk, and raise their pain.

LOOP 1: Build Certainty (Product, You, Company)

Use these rebuttals to prove the product works, you are an expert, and the company is legit. Then ask for the order again.

Element 1: Certainty in the Product

Rebuttal 1 (The Emotional "Dirty Cup" Story):

"I hear what you are saying, [Name], but let me ask you a question: Does the idea make sense to you? Do you like the idea?

"See, the true beauty of our Aegis protocol is how simply powerful it is. Let's say you didn't purchase our service. What are you left with? That outdated Google review link or QR code which your fans can use to give their praises, but at the same time, it is accessible to your haters too. And you know what the worst part is? Nobody can stop them from dragging your name, business, and revenue down. Would you like it if someone damages your business just for a small problem? Of course not, no businessman like you would like it.

"For example, let's say a customer runs into a minor, correctable issue—like a cafe customer getting a cup that was a little dirty. You understand it is a small mistake. But what if they get very upset and decide to scan your outdated QR code or click your review link, and post a bad review on Google? Once they publish it, you cannot delete it. That small mistake leaves a permanent mark on your reputation.

"This is what our Aegis is built for. For that exact same scenario, now instead of scanning that outdated link or code, they use our hi-tech protocol link or scan our code. This time they are not sent to your Google page. Instead, they are stopped by our system, and their anger is converted into private feedback that stays between you and them. They never drop a public review on Google. If we do this for all your unhappy customers, don't think this will save your reputation in the real world?"

Element 2: Certainty in You

Rebuttal 1 (The Straight-Line Reintroduction):

"Look, I completely understand the hesitation. I'm just a voice on the phone and you have no idea who I am, so let me just reintroduce myself.

"My name is Sunny Gurav, and I'm a Business Coach at Metrixmedia. I've been doing this since 2022, and I have spent my entire life learning exactly how businesses like yours operate. Just for the record, I hold degrees in both Marketing and Business Administration.

"If you're sitting there thinking I'm just trying to convince you to buy some software—I'm not. Let's be honest, I'm not going to get rich off your 1,000 rupees. My entire job is to analyze your business, find exactly where businesses like yours are bleeding money, and fix it. I pride myself on two things: I never waste my clients' time, and I deliver massive results.

"Right now, your biggest leak is simply that competitors are out-ranking you on Google. I know, without a shadow of a doubt, that if you give me just 3 minutes to show you how the Aegis Protocol works, you're going to see exactly why I called you today. Sound fair enough?"

Element 3: Certainty in the Company

Rebuttal 1 (The Aerotyne Pitch - Hungry & Elite):

"As far as our company goes, look, let me tell you who we are. The name of the company is Metrixmedia. We are a cutting-edge digital growth firm that is expanding our footprint right here in the local market. Now, because we are establishing our presence, we don't operate like those bloated, slow-moving corporate agencies that just take your money and treat you like a file on a desk. We are hungry, we are highly aggressive, and we are backing our agency with a team of elite, certified marketing specialists. Right now, our goal is to select just one or two key businesses in your neighborhood and turn them into massive success stories to showcase our results. When you partner with Metrixmedia, you aren't getting some automated system; you are getting our team's full undivided attention and creative muscle. We refuse to lose for our partners. Sound fair enough?"

"All I am asking is this: if you don't want to go with the cheapest option, then settle for the half-yearly plan, where you will save the second-most money. Fair enough?"
[THE LOOP 1 CLOSE] -> "So based on all of that, let's just get you set up today. Sound fair enough?"

LOOP 2: Lower the Action Threshold

If they still object after Loop 1, their Action Threshold is too high. First, emotionally re-pitch the 3 Certainties to get them to a 10, then remove all risk to lower their Action Threshold.

Part 1: Emotional Re-Pitch of the 3 Certainties (Product, You, Company)

This combined pitch uses Rebuttals 2 (The Employee mistake + Student Coach + Partnership Vibe) to rebuild trust and address the objection.

"Look, let's step back for a second and think about what this actually does for you. You can’t be in your business twenty-four hours a day. What if one of your employees has a bad day and gives a customer some attitude? It’s a human mistake, but what if he decides to rate it one star and writes a scathing review about 'terrible service'? Now that rating is permanently stamped on your business. With Aegis, instead of that customer blasting you publicly, our system intercepts it. It routes their complaint straight to your private email, so you can correct things internally and the public never sees a thing.

"And the reason I'm bringing this to you is because, as a business and marketing student, I didn't study how companies operate just to sell software. I do this because I hate seeing hard-working local owners bleed money to giant corporate competitors just because of an unfair Google ranking. My only job as a Business Coach is to find where your business is losing money and plug that leak.

"And I can stand behind that because at Metrixmedia, we don't do one-off deals and walk away. That's what those giant, lazy agencies do. Our entire business model is built on long-term relationships—we want to work with your business for the next ten or fifteen years. And the only way we achieve that is by delivering massive results for you right from day one. We only succeed when you win. You give us just one percent of your trust, and we will earn the remaining 99 percent. It's that simple."

Part 2: Lowering the Action Threshold (Worst Case / Best Case Close)

"What's the worst thing that can happen? Let's say you setup our software, and let's say it fails to work and doesn't intercept the bad reviews. Are you going to lose all your customers? No. Are you going to go broke by spending that 1,000 rupees? Of course not. You can cancel the subscription anytime you want, and we offer a 7-day, no-questions-asked, 100% money-back guarantee.

"But what if our system actually delivers—which is already proven? You get immediate control over your online rating. Your rating starts to climb up quickly. That new 5-star rating will start attracting new customers to your business, which directly means your revenue is going to go up.

"So the worst case is you cancel our service, get all your money back, and your business runs as if nothing happened. And if it works—which it does—your profits go up instantly. It is literally nothing to lose and everything to gain. It becomes a completely risk-free investment. Sound fair enough?"
[THE LOOP 2 CLOSE] -> "So with absolutely zero risk on your end, let's just do this. Sound fair enough?"

LOOP 3: Raise the Pain Threshold

If they object a 3rd time, they aren't feeling enough pain. Remind them of the daily revenue they are bleeding to competitors, then go for the final close.

Rebuttal 1 (The Unreasonable Customer & Action Close):

"Look, think about the most frustrating thing in business—the completely unreasonable customer. You do everything perfectly, but they leave a fake 1-star review just because they want a discount or had a bad day. Google won't take it down, and it stays there permanently, destroying your hard work. It's incredibly unfair.

"And the only reason I'm on this phone is because, as a business and marketing student, I didn't study how companies operate just to watch honest business owners get bullied online. I saw exactly where your competitors are stealing your customers, and my only goal is to help you block those fake hits and plug that leak.

"At Metrixmedia, we don't ask you to trust us on faith—we ask you to judge us on our actions. Give us just one opportunity to shield your business, and I promise you'll be a partner of ours for life. It's that simple."

Rebuttal 2 (The Emotional & Financial Close):

"Look, you told me earlier that it feels absolutely terrible when you work so hard, only for someone to leave a bad review and drag you down. And you are 100% right—it is incredibly unfair. You put your heart and soul into this business every single day, but right now, you are letting a vocal, angry minority dictate how the world sees your business. And it's not just a blow to your pride; it is actively costing you money. Every single day that goes by, potential customers are searching Google, seeing those bad reviews, and quietly going to spend their money with your competitors. You never even see them. Can you really afford to keep bleeding customers and letting people attack your hard work when the fix is right here in front of you?"
[THE FINAL LOOP 3 CLOSE] -> "Let's stop the bleeding right now. We'll set it up in 5 minutes, and if you don't love it, you get your money back. Fair enough?"

Quick Tips for Cold Calling

  • Best times to call: Usually mid-morning (10:00 AM - 11:30 AM) or early afternoon (1:30 PM - 3:00 PM) when the initial morning rush or lunch rush is over.
  • Stand up and smile: It sounds cliché, but your energy translates over the phone. Owners deal with people all day; they respond well to high, positive energy.
  • Do your homework: Make sure you actually have looked at their Google profile before calling so you know exactly how many reviews they have and what their star rating is.
